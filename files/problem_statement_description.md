## Template Structure for Business Problem Statements

### **Business Problem**

**Summary:** This section describes the core issue impacting the organization, framed in measurable terms (e.g., customer loss rates, financial impact, inefficiencies). It identifies the urgency and risk if the problem is not addressed (financial, reputational, operational).

### **Benefit of a Solution**

**Summary:** This outlines the expected value if the problem is solved. It highlights the positive outcomes for the business, such as revenue retention, cost reduction, customer satisfaction, or productivity improvements.

### **Analytics Approach**

**Summary:** This section specifies how analytics or data-driven methods will be applied to address the problem. It usually includes predictive, prescriptive, or diagnostic approaches, and briefly describes techniques or methodologies that will be employed.

### **Success Metrics**

**Summary:** This section defines how success will be measured. It typically includes quantitative goals (e.g., percentage increases, cost savings, productivity gains) and the evaluation timeframe. It makes clear what “success” means for the project.

### **Scope**

**Summary:** This section details what will be delivered as part of the project. It clarifies boundaries by describing the concrete outputs (e.g., models, metrics, processes, integrations). It also establishes what is excluded unless formally added later.

### **Details**

**Summary:** This provides operational details such as timeline, deadlines, resources, and high-level project plan. It ensures alignment around feasibility and expectations for completion.

## Typical Additions in Production Environments

In real-world professional templates, you would often see these additional sections:

* **Stakeholders & Roles** – Who is sponsoring, who is responsible, and who will be impacted.
* **Dependencies & Constraints** – External systems, data sources, or other projects that might affect success.
* **Risks & Mitigation Plans** – Potential challenges (technical, business, or operational) and strategies to reduce their impact.
* **Assumptions** – Foundational assumptions (e.g., data availability, resource allocation) that the problem statement depends on.
* **Budget & Resources** – Estimated costs, staffing, and technology needed.
* **Change Management & Adoption Plan** – How the solution will be rolled out and adopted by end users.
